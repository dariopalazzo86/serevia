Republic of Sarevia: situation brief

Read this brief as a team. Click a region on the map for its details. Everything in this exercise is fictional.

Eighteen months ago, the Government of Sarevia and the Northern Liberation Front (NLF) signed the Halden Peace Agreement, ending six years of armed conflict. The agreement promised national elections, the cantonment and reintegration of former fighters, and a reformed, accountable security sector. Elections are now scheduled in five months.

Implementation is behind schedule. A splinter faction, the NLF-Renewal (NLF-R), rejected the agreement and holds parts of the northern mining belt around Doru, financing itself through illegal gold extraction. In the east, about 48,000 internally displaced people live in three sites around Ebba, near the border with Malvane, where clashes between Aru farmers and Tamboli herders have flared since the rains failed.

In the west, 2,100 former combatants wait in cantonment sites near Lerin. Reintegration payments are three months late and NLF-R recruiters are active. In the capital, Halden, the government faces growing anger over living costs, and the main opposition party, Sarevia Forward, accuses the electoral commission of bias.

The national police are under-resourced and distrusted in the east. The national army has been accused of abuses in past operations, which places any UN support to it under the Human Rights Due Diligence Policy.

Mandate

Security Council resolution 2950 (fictional), Chapter VII. The Council decides that UNMISA’s priority tasks are:
  1. Protection of civilians under threat of physical violence, with particular attention to displaced people, women and children.
  2. Good offices and support to implementation of the Halden Peace Agreement, including disarmament, demobilization and reintegration.
  3. Support to security sector reform and to the restoration of state authority and the rule of law.
  4. Monitoring, investigating and reporting on human rights violations and abuses.
  5. Technical and logistical support to the electoral process, upon request of national authorities.

Mission at a glance

UNMISA is led by a Special Representative of the Secretary-General (SRSG). The Force has five infantry battalions and a quick reaction company; the Police component has six formed police units and individual police officers organized into colocation teams; civilian sections include Political, Civil Affairs, Human Rights, Electoral Affairs and DDR. Mission Support runs the logistics hub at Corran port, the country’s only fuel import point.

Your team plays the Integrated Planning Team reporting to the Chief of Staff. Agree roles before you start: team lead, Force, Police, Civil Affairs or Political, Human Rights, and Mission Support.
